## Example: the difference between right arithmetic and the right answer

Suppose supplies cost $100 in July and $50 in August. Answering “all expenses were $10,000” can be mathematically correct and still fail a question about supplies. Similarly, returning July $100 and August $50 does not finish “show both months and their difference.” The correct response needs both scoped totals and the $50 decrease.

Daybook keeps the database and arithmetic deterministic. The uncertain part is interpreting the question and choosing enough work to finish it. That is why the evaluation checks original requirements, account/date/source identity and visible outputs rather than trusting the model's own declaration that it is done.

## Correction memory in plain language

If the AI suggests Office supplies for paper and the user chooses Printing instead, **posting the reviewed entry** saves a useful example. Saving an unposted draft alone does not teach. The next suggestion can receive that valid example. Reversing, superseding or forgetting it removes its teaching effect. This is retrieving examples for the model; it does not retrain the model's weights. The database/browser integration and narrow selector study test different parts of that workflow and are not combined into an invented end-to-end accuracy percentage.
