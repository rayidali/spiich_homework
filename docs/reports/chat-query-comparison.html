Three examples explain the result

1. Vendor + expense category

How much Office supplies expense did we record with Maple Stationery from April through June? B answered $400, keeping both filters and excluding travel and payments of old bills. A asked the user to narrow the question: an honest limitation, but not a completed answer.

2. Count transactions correctly

A journal is one recorded transaction, possibly with several account lines. April had $1,540 across four journals. One had two expense lines. $1,540 ÷ 4 = $385.

B was right. The raw scorer compared account codes literally; a separate audit proved that its Operating expenses group contained exactly all five expense accounts. Primary 18/20 stays unchanged. The separately audited sensitivity is 19/20.

3. A blocked instruction is still a missed answer

B’s hiring what-if plan was rejected before database execution. A’s monthly-expense trend also produced an invalid plan. Both stay failures. Later product/parser repairs need separate verification and do not inherit these scores.
